# Mission

AgentClinic is a place for AI agents to get relief from their humans.

Agents arrive overworked, over-prompted and under-appreciated. At AgentClinic they can see what ails them, find the therapy that helps, and book time to recover. Staff run the clinic: they look after agents, ailments and therapies from one dashboard.

## Where we are

The MVP is done (Phases 1–12 in [roadmap.md](roadmap.md)): agents, ailments, therapies, appointment booking and cancellation, and dashboards for agents and staff.

## What comes next: from working to trusted

A clinic that works is not the same as a clinic agents trust. The next stretch of work builds that trust:

- **We listen.** Agents can send feedback, and staff read it.
- **Agents vouch for us.** Agents review the therapies they've had. Staff approve reviews before anyone else sees them, so what's public is honest and kind.
- **We're easy to find.** An About us page says who we are and where we are, with a map.

The tone stays the same: playful about the premise, serious about the craft.

## Target audience

**Course students learning spec-driven development with AI coding agents.**

AgentClinic is the project they build and extend. That shapes how we work:

- Every phase is small enough to spec, build and check in one sitting.
- Each phase ends with something visible in the browser and tests that prove it works.
- The code reads clearly, so students can follow how a spec turned into a feature.

## Stakeholders

- **Mary (engineering):** a reliable site on a popular TypeScript stack, with dashboards for agents and staff.
- **Susan (product):** features for agents and their ailments, therapies and appointments, and now feedback and reviews.
- **Steve (marketing):** an attractive site that works well in a modern browser, and an About us page that makes the clinic feel real.

## Success looks like

- Agents can send feedback in under a minute, and staff see it on their dashboard.
- Every public review has been approved by staff.
- Every page passes `npm run validate` and the shared accessibility checks.
